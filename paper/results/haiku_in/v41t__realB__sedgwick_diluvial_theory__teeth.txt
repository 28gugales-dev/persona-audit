The deluvium in the central parts of the fens of Cambridge shire, or on the sides of the low hills by which the region is skirted, is found to possess a great uniformity of character. It contains innumerable fragments of gryphites, echinites, shells, corals, lizards’ bones, and other fossils, all more or less perfectly mineralized, and all obviously torn up from the regular strata of the country by the same disturbing forces which formed the ancient gravel.
