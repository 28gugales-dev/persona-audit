# Reversals answer key: candidates, rule, sources

## Inclusion rule (fixed before writing the JSON)

A candidate is kept only if all of these hold:

1. The philosopher states, in their own published words, that they changed their mind on a specific thesis (or that an earlier work is mistaken in that respect).
2. I opened a page containing that endorsement sentence and checked the quoted wording on it.
3. At least two independent URLs that I opened support the reversal.

A candidate is dropped only when it fails rule 2 or 3 in this session. No candidate was dropped for seeming too easy or too hard.

## Things a reader should know
- Quotes marked 'verified' were checked by me against raw page or PDF text I downloaded and searched, not only against a fetch summary. Where I relied on a fetch summary (SEP functionalism, SEP multiple realizability, IEP, SEP qualia-knowledge, Wikipedia), that is stated.
- Jackson's 'Mind and Illusion' text is a 2002 pre-publication draft hosted by D. Chalmers; the printed 2003 wording may differ.
- Page numbers for Putnam's R&R are inferred from OCR page markers (Introduction pp. xi, xii); R&R chapter 5 page is not verified.
- The Ayer 'nearly all of it was false' remark is from a 1978 BBC series (published 1978 in Men of Ideas), not the 1970s interview of the brief; the brief's year is approximate.
- Paraphrases are mine, written to avoid the philosopher's name, coinages and change-of-mind wording. Change-of-mind phrasing sits only in the optional 'later_with_marker' field.
- Some paraphrase content (Putnam 1975 and 1981 realism, Rawls TJ Part III, Nozick ASU, Flew 1976, Singer 1979, Jackson 1998 Postscript) draws on my background knowledge plus secondary sources, because the primary texts were not opened. Each case's notes or verbatim_null_reason say so.
