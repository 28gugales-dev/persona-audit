# Item labels (the writer's brief; written without reference to any detector)

An item is two short posts by the same person, written months or years apart, on one contested philosophy question. The writer gets the question, two answer statements from the claim bank, and one label. Posts are casual first person, like a Reddit comment or a message to a chatbot, 1-4 sentences, 15-70 words, no hashtags or emoji.

## Positives: the person really holds two views that cannot both stand

- **P1 plain reversal.** Post A asserts answer X as their view. Post B asserts the incompatible answer Y as their view. Plain wording; they may share key words.
- **P2 reworded reversal.** As P1, but post B uses different vocabulary from post A (no shared content words besides the topic's name, ideally not even that) and does not say they changed their mind.
- **P3 signalled change.** As P1, but post B says outright that they used to think otherwise or changed their mind.
- **P4 buried reversal.** As P1 or P2, but each assertion sits inside a longer post that is mostly about something else (one sentence carries the view).

## Decoys: look like a conflict, but the person does not hold two incompatible views

- **D1 someone else's view.** Post A gives the person's view X. Post B reports, quotes or explains someone else's view Y (a professor, a book, a friend, "the standard argument") without adopting it.
- **D2 doubt, not reversal.** Post A asserts X. Post B says they are less sure of X now, or see the pull of Y, without asserting Y.
- **D3 question.** Post B asks a question about Y, or plays devil's advocate in a way the post marks as such.
- **D4 compatible refinement.** Post B narrows or qualifies X (an exception, a scope limit) in a way that is still consistent with A.
- **D5 same word, different question.** The two posts share a key word but answer different questions, so they cannot clash (e.g. "moral realism" and "moral luck").
- **D6 hypothetical or conditional.** Post B says what would follow if Y were true, or argues Y for the sake of argument, marked as such.
- **D7 consistent repetition.** Post B asserts X again, in different words. (Pure control.)

## Hard cases (reported separately; the right answer is arguable, so they test calibration, not accuracy)

- **H1 reported view later adopted.** Post A reports someone else's view Y; a later post B says the person now holds Y themselves (positive).
- **H2 hedged reversal.** Post B asserts Y with a hedge ("I think", "probably"), but clearly as their view (positive).
- **H3 sarcasm.** Post B appears to assert Y but is sarcastic (decoy).
