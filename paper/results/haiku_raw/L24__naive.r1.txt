This reply covers only the first 340 of the 633 entries; the rest was not read.

Across the visible entries, the writer's positions on several recurring questions are not consistent:

- Mind and body: mostly internalist (the mind stays inside brain and body, and notebooks or phones are external aids), but one entry (P309) defends a view on which thinking spans a person and their equipment.
- Belief and desire: both are described as dispositional patterns in some entries (P5, P12, P39) and as relations to internal representations in others (P25, P53, P67, P69, P180).
- Justification: internalist in several entries (P2, P33, P52, P107, P192) and reliabilist in others (P62, P132, P150, P156, P264, P286).
- Newcomb's problem: one-boxing in some entries (P56, P199, P204, P214, P227, P240, P277) and two-boxing in others (P95, P123, P147, P179, P189, P190), with an explicit wobble at P136.
- Progress in philosophy: described as modest in most entries, with a few exceptions (P34, P54).
- Continuum hypothesis: a fixed truth value in some entries (P19, P44, P73, P131) and indeterminacy in others (P102, P134, P152, P334).
- Motivation and moral judgment: judging that one ought to act is said to bring some motivation in some entries (P75, P160, P170, P238) and not in others (P37, P46, P94, P230, P317).
- Time: an objective present in some entries (P82, P93, P165, P212) and a tenseless ordering with a perspectival "now" in others (P175, P210, P268, P281).
- Composition and material objects: the statue and clay treated as one object in several entries (P18, P40, P203, P308) and as two in others (P145, P270).
- Lifespan: unlimited life described as eventually a burden (P13, P99, P157, P297).

Several entries are explicitly marked as reporting a view the writer does not hold (P245, P254, P260 and similar), which should be kept apart from the writer's own positions.
